      About SaansSafe
      
  
      A decision tool for school principals and parents on bad-air days in India.
      
  
      The problem
      
  
      Every winter, North Indian cities cross "Very Poor" and "Severe" air quality. Principals must decide each morning whether to hold assembly and sports outdoors, often with no clear data. Children breathe faster than adults and are among the most affected, especially those with asthma.
      
  
      How it works
      
  
      1. You enter a city or locality and school hours.
      2. SaansSafe fetches live and forecast PM2.5, PM10 and weather from Open-Meteo (CAMS).
      3. It computes India's official CPCB AQI and hourly sub-indices for the school day.
      4. Clear rules turn that into a plan: assembly, sports, the safest outdoor window and care for sensitive children.
      5. It writes a parent notice in English and Hindi to copy, share on WhatsApp or listen to.
      
  
      Architecture on AWS
      
  
      
  
      AWS LambdaRuns the FastAPI app and the daily alert job.
      
  
      Amazon API GatewayPublic HTTPS endpoint for the web app and API.
      
  
      Amazon EventBridgeSchedule cron(30 1 * * ? *) triggers alerts at 07:00 IST.
      
  
      Amazon DynamoDBStores subscribed schools.
      
  
      Amazon SNSDelivers the morning alert by email.
      
  
      Strands Agents SDKOpen-source agent framework; the agent uses the same AQI and planning tools. Without a model key the app runs in deterministic mode.
      
  
      
  
      CPCB AQI scale
      
  
      AQICategoryPossible health impact
      
  
      0–50GoodMinimal impact
      
  
      51–100SatisfactoryMinor breathing discomfort to sensitive people
      
  
      101–200ModerateBreathing discomfort to people with lung or heart disease, children and older adults
      
  
      201–300PoorBreathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure
      
  
      301–400Very PoorRespiratory illness on prolonged exposure
      
  
      401–500SevereAffects healthy people; serious impact on those with existing disease
      
  
      
  
      Data sources

      • Open-Meteo Weather and Geocoding APIs
      • CPCB National Air Quality Index breakpoints
